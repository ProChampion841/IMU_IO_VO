PLAN
====
Next experiment, one change at a time.

THESIS: there are confirmed geometry defects that must be corrected before the
body-rate ablation can be interpreted. Whether geometry FULLY explains the
lateral failure remains an experimental question, not a settled one.

runs/vo_metrics.csv is the REAL flight, trained on the A100 and copied here by
hand. The checkpoint is still on the server.

CAUTION ON EVERY RATE NUMBER BELOW: yaw and roll rates quoted here were measured
on data/ (the SYNTHETIC capture), because the real flight is not on this machine.
They must be re-measured on the real flight before any of the magnitudes are
trusted. Section 15 lists what that changes.


1. WHAT THE LOG SAYS ABOUT THE AIRCRAFT
----------------------------------------
1.1  Per-axis mean-predictor floors on the real validation split, from the CSV:
     forward 3.530, lateral 2.915, vertical 1.335 m/s. These check out exactly
     against the recorded overall 4.768.
1.2  Transverse spread 3.206 m/s over a direction spread of 8.100 deg gives a
     mean airspeed of about 22.7 m/s. Derived, not assumed.
1.3  The lateral axis therefore varies by about 7.4 deg of crab about the mean.
1.4  At 22.7 m/s that is wind, not sideslip, and the capture has a wind column.
1.5  Wind crab cannot be predicted from attitude. It depends on the wind vector,
     which no input carries. Only the camera sees it, because only the camera
     measures motion relative to the GROUND.
1.6  So the lateral axis stands or falls on the frontend, not on the aiding stream.


2. CONFIRMED AND LARGEST: THE ROTATION CORRECTION IS ONE CONSTANT PER IMAGE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  RotationalSearchCenter is nn.Linear(3, 2, bias=False), so it emits ONE
     (dx, dy) per image (vision_mamba_vo.py:81, :107).
2.2  LocalCorrelation broadcasts it over every cell with
     center.view(B,2,1,1).expand(B,2,H,W) (correlation.py:91). At 576x1024 and
     patch 8 that is one pair of numbers replicated into 9,216 cells.
2.3  Real rotational flow is not constant. In normalised coordinates:
       u = x*y*wx - (1+x^2)*wy + y*wz
       v = (1+y^2)*wx -  x*y*wy - x*wz
2.4  A constant removes exactly the order-0 terms, -wy in u and +wx in v. That
     is all it can ever remove.
2.5  The curl term (+y*wz, -x*wz) is ODD about the principal point, so its mean
     over a symmetric image is EXACTLY zero. The best constant approximation is
     the zero vector, so NONE of the curl is removed. This is structural.
2.6  Magnitude at the image corner (r_n = 0.575 from the real intrinsics,
     f_working 1052, dt 0.05 s), using SYNTHETIC-flight yaw rates:
       yaw  6.11 deg/s RMS  -> 3.23 px = 0.403 cells = 2.1x the crab signal
       yaw 11.52 deg/s peak -> 6.08 px = 0.760 cells = 4.0x the crab signal
2.7  The horizontal component alone is 0.205 cells RMS, which by itself equals
     the entire 0.191-cell crab signal.
2.8  In velocity units at 100 m: corner curl 6.15 m/s RMS, 11.6 m/s peak.
2.9  It is turn-correlated by construction, which is exactly the signature the
     lateral investigation is chasing.
2.10 It is NOT clipped by the search window: 1.49 forward + 0.76 curl stays
     inside radius 4. So it is measured and reported as translation.
2.11 Pooling does NOT cancel it. token_projection holds an independent weight
     per (channel, cell) over cell_dim = (9 + grid_dim) * 36, so the 36 cells
     are never averaged into one number. Zero-mean-ness describes an average
     the model never takes. Each block's own mean is 5/6 of its corner value.
2.12 Worse, the occupied mask hard-zeroes any block below min_pool_weight, so a
     texture dropout can delete one arm of the curl outright and leave the other.
2.13 A half-image dropout at peak yaw yields 0.193 cells = 2.945 m/s, which is
     101% of the crab signal, produced purely as a function of yaw rate.
2.14 The audit of the real capture records detail_persistence 0.486, "fine detail
     is weak; matching will be unreliable". Dropout is the expected regime, not
     an edge case.
2.15 THE FIX IS CHEAP. LocalCorrelation ALREADY accepts a per-cell centre field:
     correlation.py:92 permits (B,2,H,W) and :131 reads the centre per pixel.
     Nothing in the repo ever supplies one. Evaluating the full u/v field on the
     feature grid removes the curl and the quadratic terms geometrically, with
     no change to the correlator, the pooling, or the fusion contract.
2.16 The normalised (x, y) per cell are already derivable from _bearing_scale.


3. CAMERA MOUNTING IS ASSUMED, NEVER MEASURED, AND THE TOOL IS MISSING
------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  The architecture asserts a nadir camera: log_speed = log_rate + log_altitude
     is correct only when the optical axis points at the ground.
3.2  But no camera-to-body rotation exists anywhere. The calibration's mounting
     block holds only gps_to_camera_m, and README says the omission is deliberate.
3.3  tools/calibrate_camera_imu_rotation.py, which seed_from_jacobian names and
     train_fixedwing_vo.py:1497 requires, DOES NOT EXIST in tools/.
3.4  So --rotation-map can never be used and the rotation map always starts from
     zeros. A documented capability that cannot be exercised.
3.5  The mounting does NOT change the conclusion in section 2. On the synthetic
     data roll is 7.06 deg/s RMS against yaw 6.12, so under a forward-facing
     camera the curl axis becomes roll and the curl GROWS to 0.466 cells RMS.
3.6  Both mountings leave an un-removable curl larger than the crab signal.


4. THE LATERAL SIGNAL IS SMALLER THAN ONE CORRELATION CELL
-----------------------------------------------------------
4.1  Real calibration: fx 1973.0, fy 1965.4 on 1920x1080. At 576x1024 working
     size that is 1052 px, so one 8 px cell subtends 7.6 mrad.
4.2  Flow per image pair, in cells, at 20 Hz (dt = 0.05 s):

       altitude   forward 22.7 m/s   crab 2.92 m/s   1 deg of direction
          50 m          2.99             0.383            0.052
         100 m          1.49             0.192            0.026
         200 m          0.75             0.096            0.013

4.3  To get direction right to 1 deg at 100 m the correlator must resolve 0.026
     of a cell, which is 0.39 of a NATIVE pixel.
4.4  The model's current 8 deg direction error is about 0.21 cells at 100 m.
4.5  Forward velocity learns because its signal is 1.5 cells and lives in the
     integer argmax. Crab does not because 0.19 cells lives entirely in the
     sub-cell refinement, where section 2's curl is 2 to 4 times larger.
4.6  UNKNOWN AND NEEDED: the flight's actual AGL range. Every row scales as 1/h.


5. LENS DISTORTION: REAL, BUT SMALLER THAN FIRST STATED
--------------------------------------------------------
5.1  The lens has k1 = -0.393. Local flow magnification over the real sensor
     area is mean 0.896, sigma 0.068, range 0.711 to 1.000.
5.2  The MEAN is a constant and the log_rate head absorbs it. Only the SPREAD
     is unlearnable: about +/- 7.6% at one sigma, with the central 90% of pixels
     spanning 24% and the corner reaching -29%.
5.3  Correct wording: uncorrected distortion introduces up to about 26-29%
     position-dependent flow error near the image boundary, and about 7.6% at
     one sigma over the frame. Not a 26% whole-image error.
5.4  Against a 0.19-cell crab signal, 7.6% of the 1.49-cell forward flow is
     0.11 cells, about 60% of the signal. Material but second to section 2.
5.5  THE WIRING BUG IS REAL. VisualPairSource accepts a distortion vector and
     builds cv2.initUndistortRectifyMap, folding undistortion and resize into
     one remap. But build_vo_dataset has no distortion parameter and the trainer
     never reads calibration.distortion, so the path is unreachable from training.
5.6  Note for future reviewers: it is easy to read image_pairs.py and conclude
     distortion IS corrected. The capability exists; the wiring does not. This is
     the same capability-versus-use trap as section 2.15, in the other direction.


6. THE 77% ANISOTROPY CLAIM IS CONDITIONAL AND UNPROVEN
--------------------------------------------------------
6.1  Without --calibration the bearing scale falls back to 1/image_size, giving a
     vertical-to-horizontal ratio of 1.778 against a true 1.004.
6.2  WITH --calibration the frontend uses resized fx and fy and the error is 0.4%.
6.3  It was inferred that the fallback was in use because commands.txt passes no
     --calibration. commands.txt is documentation, not the command that ran.
6.4  HOW TO SETTLE IT: the trainer logs "camera: WxH fx=.. fy=.." if and only if
     a calibration loaded. Grep the server's training log for that line.
6.5  Note the two are independent: passing --calibration fixes the bearing scale
     but does NOT fix distortion, because of 5.5.
6.6  This affects the m/s conversions above but not any cell count, which are
     all calibration-independent.


7. FRAME GAP: CANNOT BE DECIDED FROM ALTITUDE ALONE
-----------------------------------------------------
7.1  --frame-gap 2 doubles the interval to 0.1 s, so all flow doubles and the
     sub-cell requirement halves. One flag, no code, no extra compute.
7.2  Forward flow in cells at gap 2, by altitude and speed:

       altitude   22.7 m/s   25 m/s   30 m/s      (radius is 4)
          75 m      3.98      4.38     5.26
         100 m      2.99      3.29     3.95
         150 m      1.99      2.19     2.63

7.3  So 75 m is already over the window at 25 m/s. An earlier "safe above 75 m"
     claim was wrong.
7.4  Height above TERRAIN is what matters, not RelativeAlt, which is height above
     the takeoff point. Note that aiding_features has an altitude_offset_m
     parameter for exactly this and no caller ever passes it.
7.5  Search-window use also depends on peak ground speed, focal length, pair
     interval, camera orientation, and the rotational-compensation error of
     section 2. No table settles it.
7.6  DECIDE IT EMPIRICALLY: log how often the correlation peak lands on the
     search boundary. That captures every factor above at once and is the right
     test. Do not set frame gap from altitude alone.
7.7  Other resolution levers, which multiply with frame gap: --patch-size 4
     doubles cells per unit flow at 4x correlation compute; --image-size
     1080 1920 gives 1.875x at 3.5x compute.


8. VISUAL INFORMATION IS ~370 ms OLD, NOT 40 ms
------------------------------------------------
8.1  --deployment-latency-s delays the VISUAL stream only, at image_pairs.py:206.
8.2  It does not shift the target. Aiding, altitude and target are sliced at the
     same tick indices, and the trainer asserts those clocks match.
8.3  At the predicted tick, attitude and altitude are 0 ms old and the freshest
     image is 350-390 ms old, mean 370 ms. Measured, not assumed.
8.4  The earlier "40 ms" was only the 20 Hz image spacing with the pipeline delay
     silently set to zero. It was wrong.
8.5  Keep visual measurement age in the diagnostics.


9. WARMUP: TWO DIFFERENT CASES, ONLY ONE IS BROKEN
----------------------------------------------------
9.1  --warmup is 20 ticks (200 ms).
9.2  COLD START, meaning the first window of a split range and every horizon-eval
     span: no visual event has been delivered yet, so the model is blind for
     35-40 ticks and a 20-tick warmup does not cover it.
9.3  ORDINARY TRAINING WINDOWS are NOT blind. They can receive events captured
     before the window began, provided those events stay inside the same split.
9.4  So an earlier claim that every span is blind for 35-40 ticks was too broad.
9.5  Treat the two separately. Only the cold-start case needs a longer warmup or
     an exclusion of blind ticks from scoring.


10. THE OVERFITTING DIAGNOSIS DOES NOT HOLD UP
-----------------------------------------------
10.1 The argument was "train improves while val degrades, so it overfits".
10.2 The train/val ratio NARROWS: 0.636 at epochs 2-4 to 0.777 at epochs 9-12.
10.3 So the CSV does not discriminate overfitting from any other cause. Withdrawn.
10.4 The LR column cannot help either: 0.000300 for all 12 epochs is consistent
     with any T_max at or above 424, so it cannot even prove the anneal ran.
10.5 Step count per epoch on the real dataset must be read from the server log.


11. THE LATERAL AXIS IS STILL THE STRONGEST SIGNAL IN THE LOG
--------------------------------------------------------------
11.1 At epoch 12 the train/val gap decomposes as x 4.2%, y 92.6%, z 3.3%.
11.2 y carries 67-93% of the squared gap in every epoch from 2 onward.
11.3 Dropout enters nine shared residual branches, so its noise should land about
     equally on y and z. The observed asymmetry is 6:1 after normalising.
11.4 On training data y improved only 10% while x improved 74% and z 37%.
11.5 Overfitting cannot explain a training-set failure. Sections 2 and 4 give a
     mechanism that can.


12. WHY val LOOKS EASIER THAN train: TWO LIVE CANDIDATES
----------------------------------------------------------
12.1 train_vel_rmse is measured with dropout ON and averaged over an epoch of
     changing weights. val_vel_rmse is dropout OFF on end-of-epoch weights.
12.2 There is also no train-side mean-predictor floor anywhere in the code.
12.3 Mid-epoch averaging is about 2% of the gap. Masking and windowing are zero.
12.4 The val_baseline_* columns cannot settle it: they measure distance to the
     TRAINING mean, an upper bound on validation spread, never a lower one.
12.5 ONE LINE SETTLES IT: run evaluate() over loaders["train"] with the same
     baseline. Isolates dropout, weight state and the missing floor at once.


13. KAPPA AND THE DIRECTION LOSS
---------------------------------
13.1 kappa is computed on every batch and thrown away. It has no consumer.
13.2 Logging the MEAN kappa per epoch is about four one-line edits.
13.3 Per-tick kappa, yaw rate, visual age and direction error with stratified
     aggregation is NOT four lines. It needs metadata propagation through the
     window and a real aggregation path. Budget it as a proper change.
13.4 Mean kappa can also be recovered approximately from the existing CSV by
     inverting the loss against the reported direction error: about 34 rising
     to 42 across the 12 epochs, and from epoch 4 the loss beats the constant
     kappa bound, which proves kappa is genuinely per-sample.
13.5 What that does NOT show is WHICH samples get low kappa.
13.6 The lever-arm confound is withdrawn. Measured yaw rate never exceeds
     20 deg/s, so at a 1.5 m offset the label error is 0.40 deg RMS, which moves
     optimal kappa by 0.25%. GPSNavEul is also a unit-converted copy of NavEul
     to 5e-7 rad, so the GPS-yaw mechanism does not exist here either.
13.7 Do not touch the direction loss until kappa diagnostics justify it.


14. PROTOCOL FOR ANY A/B
-------------------------
14.1 CosineAnnealingLR takes T_max from --epochs, so the budget IS an optimiser
     setting. Two arms with different --epochs are different optimisers.
14.2 best.pt takes a running minimum, so a longer arm wins on order statistics.
14.3 A and B must use IDENTICAL --epochs, schedule and seeds-per-arm.
14.4 Early stopping does NOT break this. It is a selection rule and does not
     alter the trajectory before it fires. An earlier objection was wrong.
14.5 Ranks do not diverge under a plateau schedule: evaluate() all-reduces before
     computing metrics. That objection was also wrong.
14.6 Budget in optimiser steps, not epochs, and state the step count.
14.7 Epoch-to-epoch variation is NOT a noise estimate. Three seeds per arm is to
     ESTIMATE the seed spread, which is currently unknown. Report it.

16. STILL UNKNOWN
-----------------
16.1 The real flight's yaw rate, roll rate, AGL range and peak ground speed.
     Everything in sections 2, 4 and 7 is conditional on these.
16.2 Whether --calibration was passed on the server run.
16.3 The real airframe's camera mounting. Undocumented by design, and the tool
     that would measure it does not exist.
16.4 Whether the learned yaw column of the rotation map is near zero. If it is
     NOT, there is a second additive failure: a turn-correlated constant
     translation approximating a field with no translational part.
16.5 How much of the train-versus-validation gap is dropout and how much is turns.
16.6 Whether low kappa lands on turns.
16.7 Whether the run overfits at all. The 12-epoch log cannot say.
16.8 On synthetic data sin(roll) correlates 0.9964 with yaw rate, so the aiding
     vector already carries a strong yaw surrogate in coordinated flight. Whether
     that holds on the real flight is unmeasured, and it weakens but does not
     remove the case for explicit body rates.
